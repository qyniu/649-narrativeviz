# Narrative Revisions for Scenes 3, 4, and 7

## Scene 3: The Narrowing Path — Declining Odds
### (Before Scene 3: The Mathematics of Survival)
When the sea of supply rises but the shores of attention remain fixed, the pressure falls entirely on the individual creator. The "attention gap" we saw in Scene 2 is not an abstract economic theory; it is a brutal filter applied to every game launched.

The visualizations below unpack the "Scissors Effect"—where soaring competition and flat engagement intersect to sever the odds of success. In a single decade, the likelihood of a game reaching a baseline of 1,000 reviews plummeted by an astonishing factor of twenty-two. But more revealing is the shifting anatomy of a hit: as the odds shrink, the market increasingly rewards games with higher price points and insists on near-perfect positive reception. Breaking through is no longer about just being good; it requires an uncompromising alignment of quality and resources.

## Scene 4: The Gravity of Attention — Winner Takes All
### (Before Scene 4: The Economics of the Spotlight)
If overall attention is stagnant and the middle class is dissolving, a crucial question emerges: where did all the players go?

The answer lies in the physics of digital visibility. Attention generates more attention. The Steam algorithm, Twitch streamers, and word-of-mouth networks all compound the success of games that are already succeeding. The Lorenz curve below illustrates a terrifying reality: the market isn't just crowded; it has collapsed into a singularity.

We are witnessing the death of equitable discovery. Each passing year displays a deeper bow in the curve—a stark visual metaphor for how a microscopic fraction of elite titles now devour nearly all the oxygen in the room, leaving thousands of games gasping in their wake.

## Closing: Out of the Flood — The Survivor's Profile
### (Before Scene 7: Anatomy of the 1%)
After confronting the grim realities of the attention gap, collapsing odds, and crushing inequality, we must ask: who actually makes it out alive?

The final canvas reveals the survivors—those rare artifacts that successfully navigated the flood. What you see is not a random distribution of luck. The survivors congregate tightly in the extreme upper-right, a razor-thin corridor where uncompromising quality meets massive scale. Down below lies the gray expanse of the silent majority.

For an indie developer today, a great game is merely the price of admission. Escaping the gravitational pull of the invisible bottom demands both fierce craftsmanship and undeniable market resonance. The flood is permanent, but as these survivors prove, those who learn to build arks can still ride the wave.
